Até Zerar started in **December 2021** and has never stopped. The pitch fits
in one line: **the whole game, start to finish, with no commentary, in 4K at 60
frames per second.** The idea is to watch it like a film.

It was not the beginning. Before it came [Blucker12](/en/experiencia), from 2018
to 2021, with 921 videos of commentated gameplay, news, and speedruns. Até Zerar
was born from a decision to cut almost all of that and keep one thing.

Today the channel has **165 videos**, **26.3 thousand subscribers**, and **5.7
million views**.

## What "no commentary" changes

Commentated gameplay is YouTube's default format, and it is what almost
everyone does. Até Zerar does the opposite on purpose.

With no narration, the video stops being about the person playing and becomes
about the game. Someone looking up *God of War* to revisit the story, or someone
who wants to see a game without buying it, does not need anyone's opinion in the
middle. It is the writing, the score, and the performance of the game itself,
exactly as the studio built it.

That has a cost. There is no personality holding attention, so **capture quality
becomes everything**: resolution, frame rate, no drops, no bad cuts. A twelve
hour video does not forgive carelessness during recording.

## The scale

The archive runs from **PlayStation 1 to PlayStation 5 Pro**, plus Xbox,
Nintendo, and PC. It is organised into playlists by franchise (Crash Bandicoot,
God of War, Resident Evil, The Last of Us, Grand Theft Auto, Forza Horizon) and
also by year, which gives two ways to find the same thing.

The most watched:

| Video | Views |
| --- | --- |
| God of War 1 (PS2) | 1.3 million |
| Forza Horizon 5 (PC) | 630 thousand |
| God of War 2 (PS2) | 473 thousand |
| GTA San Andreas Definitive (PC) | 314 thousand |
| Black (PS2) | 235 thousand |
| The Last of Us Part 1 (PC) | 226 thousand |

Notice the pattern: **the winners are not the new releases**. God of War 1 and 2
are PlayStation 2 titles, and so is Black. What brings people back year after
year is old games people want to revisit and can no longer play.

And these are not short videos. Runtimes go from an hour and a half to more than
thirty hours in a single file. The complete *The Walking Dead* runs 31 hours and
45 minutes.

## What four years of consistency teach

**Production is process, not inspiration.** Record, transcode, review, upload,
write the description, build the playlist. Repeated 165 times. What sustains
that is not motivation, it is having the process built well enough to run on a
bad day too.

**An archive compounds.** A video published four years ago still brings people
in today. Unlike news content, which dies in a week, a full playthrough of a
classic game only accumulates. The archive is the product, not this week's
upload.

**File weight is an engineering problem.** Twelve hours in 4K60 is not "a
video", it is a storage, transcoding, and delivery problem. Living with that is
exactly what led to the idea of building a video platform from scratch,
[BluckerTV](/en/blog/bluckertv), and then to finding out, with numbers, why it
did not add up.

## Where it stands

Still running, with new videos going up. It is the widest-reaching work in the
collection, and together with the earlier channel it adds up to almost eight
unbroken years of publishing video. That is why it sits here alongside
everything else: it is not a hobby kept apart from the work, it is production
with an audience, a schedule, and an archive.
