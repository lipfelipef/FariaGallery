Blucker12 was my first channel, from October 2018 to December 2021. That is
three years and three months, 921 videos published, 3.2 million views, and 11.4
thousand subscribers.

It was commentated gameplay: me playing and talking over it. Guides, news,
speedruns, competitions. I held world records in Resident Evil 4.

The channel is still online, as an archive. What ended was the new production,
and this piece is about why.

## What 921 videos teach you about routine

The number I like quoting is not the view count: it is the video count. Nine
hundred and twenty-one videos in three years and three months is almost one
every day and a half, without stopping, exam weeks and holidays included.

That is not talent, it is routine. Record, cut, write the description, pick the
title, upload, publish. Repeated until it becomes automatic. I learned to edit
video because I had to edit that day, not because I sat down to study editing.

It is the same thing I would later do with code, and the order matters: the
habit of shipping came first, and only much later did the tooling get good.
Someone who ships every week improves faster than someone who studies in order
to ship well once.

## The part nobody sees in a gameplay channel

A commentated gameplay video looks like the simplest thing in the world: start
the game, start the mic, talk. It is not.

What eats the time is not the recording, it is everything around it. Choosing
what to record, because what I want to play and what someone wants to watch are
rarely the same thing. Holding a publishing rhythm when the video did not turn
out good. Finding out that the title decides more than the content, which is
frustrating and true at the same time.

And the technical part that only shows up with volume: storage. Raw gameplay is
huge, and 921 videos' worth of raw footage does not fit anywhere cheap. I
learned to delete what would not be useful again, which is a skill in itself.

## Why drop the format that was working

In December 2021 I stopped publishing on Blucker12 and started Até Zerar, which
is the same person doing the opposite: **whole games, start to finish, with no
commentary at all, in 4K60fps.**

The decision looks counterintuitive. The commentated channel had three years of
archive, 11 thousand subscribers, and an audience. Starting over in another
format throws away the accumulated advantage.

The reason is that the commentated format had a ceiling I could not break. My
commentary was not the differentiator: it was what made me the same as everyone
else. There were thousands of channels doing commentated gameplay in Portuguese,
and mine was not better than the others for any structural reason, only through
effort.

Without commentary, the comparison changes axis. What counts is the capture, the
consistency, and the silence, which is exactly what almost nobody wants to do,
because it does not show. And the video starts serving a purpose the commentated
one cannot: watching it like a film, or leaving it running.

The numbers came later and proved the decision right: Até Zerar today has 165
videos and 5.7 million views, against 921 videos and 3.2 million for Blucker12.
**Five times fewer videos and nearly double the audience.**

## What I took from it

Two things.

**First, that volume teaches faster than isolated study.** The 921 videos are
worth nothing today as an archive, but the three years of routine explain why I
can carry a project alone all the way to the end.

**Second, harder to apply than to write: knowing when what is working is no
longer what will work.** Blucker12 did not close because it failed. It closed
because I looked at what I was doing and concluded the ceiling was close, and
that the effort could be traded for one that paid better.

That is the same question that came up with [BluckerTV](/en/blog/bluckertv/)
five years later, and there the answer was to shut down without starting
anything else. Not every exit is a trade; sometimes it is just an exit.
